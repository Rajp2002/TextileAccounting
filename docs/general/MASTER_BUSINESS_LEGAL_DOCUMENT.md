# Weavly Master Business And Legal Document

Prepared: June 15, 2026

This document is a founder operating plan, not legal or tax advice. Use it to brief an Indian CA, Indian startup lawyer, Canadian CPA, and Canadian business lawyer. The goal is to turn Weavly from a semi-production app into a business that can safely run pilots, onboard paying Indian textile factories, collect revenue, protect founder equity/IP, and move profits to the founder personally through lawful channels.

## 1. Founder Context And North Star

You are a bootstrapped Canadian founder currently in India on an e-Business visa. You used modern AI tooling to compress the product lifecycle and now have a semi-finished production SaaS for Indian textile operations. A cofounder/family operator will manage on-ground operations in India when you move back to Canada.

Weavly is not a generic ERP. The current product is a multi-tenant textile operations SaaS for Indian weaving factories: costing, quotes/orders, market runs, supervisor production logs, stock, dispatches, commercials, payroll, advances, WhatsApp communication, billing, referrals, and compliance/security records.

The business objective is:

1. Run a controlled pilot with your brother/factory context without pretending the app is legally or commercially finished.
2. Convert pilot learning into a paid, implementation-led Indian SaaS offer.
3. Put the correct entity, tax, contract, privacy, payment, and support structure under the app before onboarding external clients.
4. Extract profits personally through clean tax/legal routes instead of mixing business money and personal money.

## 2. Executive Recommendation

For the next 90 days, treat Weavly as an India-first B2B SaaS with an Indian operating company and Canadian-founder ownership planning.

Recommended default path:

1. **Short pilot phase:** no public payment promises, no live Razorpay charges, no external customer onboarding without pilot paperwork and consent.
2. **Business structure:** incorporate an Indian private limited company as the operating company before charging external Indian factories. The local cofounder/operator should likely be the India-resident director/operator. Confirm foreign shareholder/director treatment with counsel.
3. **Commercial model:** move away from pure monthly SaaS and toward "factory digitisation setup + annual maintenance", consistent with `docs/pricing-strategy-and-billing-migration-guide.md`.
4. **Legal pack:** update Terms, Privacy, Refund, DPA, pilot agreement, worker/WhatsApp consent, subprocessor list, incident runbook, and retention/export policy before paid launch.
5. **Profit extraction:** use documented salary/director remuneration, dividends, founder loan repayment, reimbursed expenses, and legitimate cross-border service/IP payments where supported by contracts, tax withholding, transfer pricing, and foreign-exchange compliance.

Do not route customer money through personal accounts once external paid pilots begin. It makes GST, income tax, founder accounting, refunds, customer trust, and later fundraising/diligence much harder.

## 3. Entity Structure Options

### Option A: Indian Private Limited OpCo First

Best default for first Indian revenue.

Why it fits:

- Customers, workers, GST, Razorpay settlement, support, and on-ground onboarding are in India.
- Indian B2B factory owners are more likely to trust invoices from an Indian entity.
- Razorpay live setup, GST invoices, and local bank settlement are simpler with an Indian entity.
- The on-ground cofounder can manage implementation, training, collections, and support.

Likely setup:

- `Weavly Technologies Private Limited` or similar.
- Founder and cofounder hold shares under a founders agreement.
- At least one India-resident director is required for an Indian company. Verify the exact director/residency requirement and day count with the lawyer/CS.
- All IP created before incorporation is assigned/licensed into the company through a founder IP assignment.
- The company owns customer contracts, invoices, support obligations, and India payroll/vendor expenses.

Use this if the immediate market is Surat/Gujarat/India factories and the priority is first revenue.

### Option B: Canadian HoldCo + Indian Subsidiary

Better later if you expect Canadian fundraising, Canadian grants, foreign investors, or want Canadian top-level IP ownership.

Possible setup:

- Canadian corporation owns product IP and maybe owns most/all of Indian OpCo.
- Indian OpCo sells, implements, supports, and invoices Indian customers.
- Indian OpCo pays Canada for software license, management services, or IP use only under real contracts, with withholding tax, GST/import-of-service analysis, transfer pricing, and FEMA/authorized dealer bank process.

This is cleaner for long-term global ownership, but it is heavier. It is usually not the fastest first-revenue path unless you already have Canadian counsel/CPA and a clear investor reason.

### Option C: LLP/Partnership/Sole Proprietor

Not recommended as the main structure for Weavly.

Why:

- SaaS liability, privacy exposure, payroll/legal disclaimers, customer contracts, and cofounder equity are cleaner in a company.
- Foreign investment into LLPs is possible in some automatic-route sectors, but operationally more nuanced.
- Sole proprietor/personal collection is bad for diligence and profit tracking.

## 4. Founder, Cofounder, And IP Paperwork

Before external paid customers, create these documents:

1. **Founder agreement:** equity split, vesting, roles, decision rights, deadlock, leaver rules, share transfer limits, confidentiality, non-solicit where enforceable, dispute venue.
2. **IP assignment:** all code, docs, prompts, designs, schemas, brand assets, and product work created before incorporation must be assigned/licensed to the company.
3. **Cofounder operations agreement:** your cofounder handles onboarding, customer training, local collections support, factory relationships, and first-line support; you own product, architecture, security, growth, and Canada-side strategy.
4. **Expense and reimbursement policy:** travel, phone, devices, SaaS subscriptions, cloud costs, and support costs are reimbursed only with invoices/receipts.
5. **Founder loan record:** if you put personal money into the company, record it as equity or a founder loan. Loan repayment can return principal later, but it is not "profit".

## 5. Visa And Tax Residency Watchpoints

### India e-Business Visa

Official Indian visa guidance says e-Visa is not for employment, and e-Business visa documentation requires details of the Indian firm/company for business activities. MHA business-visa guidance includes visits to establish or explore an industrial/business venture. If your India stay on business visa exceeds 180 days in a calendar year, MHA guidance indicates business visa holders must register with FRRO/FRO.

Practical rule: use the e-Business visa for business establishment, meetings, customer discovery, pilots, hiring discussions, and setup. Do not treat it as local employment. If you need a salary from the Indian company while physically in India, get legal immigration advice first.

### India Tax Residency

India's Income Tax Department states the general individual residency test as 182 days in the financial year, or 60 days in that year plus 365 days in the preceding four years, with special exceptions for Indian citizens/PIOs. Track India day count carefully.

Practical rule:

- Keep a day-count calendar.
- Confirm whether you are Canadian-only, OCI/PIO, or Indian citizen/PIO for tax-residency exceptions.
- If you become India tax resident or RNOR/ROR, India may tax more than just India-source income.
- If you are paid by the Indian company while in India, handle Indian TDS/payroll/consulting tax properly.

### Canada Tax Residency

CRA residency depends on facts, including residential ties, time, purpose, intent, and continuity. CRA says factual residents temporarily outside Canada generally report worldwide income. If you remain Canadian resident, Indian salary/dividends/fees may need to be reported in Canada, with possible foreign tax credit for eligible foreign tax paid.

Practical rule:

- Get a Canadian CPA opinion before taking dividends/fees from India.
- Keep Indian withholding certificates and tax payment proofs.
- Do not assume "I am physically in India" means "Canada cannot tax me."

## 6. Profit Extraction Playbook

The right question is not "how do I pull money out?" It is "which legal channel matches the reason money is leaving the company?"

### Clean Channels

1. **Salary or director remuneration from Indian OpCo**
   - Good when you are actively working for the Indian company.
   - Deductible business expense if reasonable and properly approved.
   - Requires payroll/TDS and immigration review if you are physically working in India.

2. **Consulting or management fee**
   - Good if you personally or your Canadian company provides real product, engineering, sales, strategy, or management services.
   - Must have a written agreement, invoices, market-rate pricing, tax withholding, GST/import-of-service analysis, and transfer-pricing support if related-party.

3. **Dividends**
   - Good for distributing profits after corporate tax and retained working capital.
   - Not deductible to the Indian company.
   - Indian non-resident dividend taxation is subject to domestic law and DTAA. The Canada-India treaty allows source-country dividend withholding limits where conditions are met, including 15% for a company beneficial owner controlling at least 10% voting power and 25% in other cases under Article 10, subject to current law, treaty eligibility, beneficial ownership, TRC/Form 10F, and professional advice.

4. **Founder loan repayment**
   - Good for returning money you previously loaned to the company.
   - Repays principal; it is not profit.
   - Must be documented with board approval and compliant with Indian company/deposit/FEMA rules.

5. **Expense reimbursements**
   - Good for real business expenses you paid personally.
   - Needs receipts, approval, business purpose, and no personal spending disguised as business.

6. **IP royalty/license fee**
   - Only if IP ownership is genuinely outside Indian OpCo and the company licenses it.
   - Heavier: withholding, GST, transfer pricing, FEMA, and treaty analysis.
   - Do not use this casually in year one unless counsel structures it.

### Avoid

- Customer payments into personal accounts.
- Cash withdrawals treated as "founder money".
- Fake consulting invoices.
- Personal rent, family expenses, or travel booked as company expenses without business purpose.
- Paying Canada/yourself before GST, payroll, hosting, refunds, support, and taxes are covered.

### Practical First-Year Distribution Policy

Until revenue is predictable:

1. Keep 3-6 months of hosting, support, CA/lawyer, onboarding, and refund reserve in the company.
2. Reimburse documented founder expenses monthly.
3. Pay modest founder/operator compensation only when there is enough monthly recurring/implementation revenue.
4. Use dividends only after annual accounts, tax provisioning, and board/shareholder approval.
5. Use cross-border fees only after contracts and CA/CPA review.

### Critical Gaps Before Paid Launch

1. **Legal copy is stale and inconsistent**
   - Terms pricing is stale.
   - Billing/landing/onboarding/access gates disagree.
   - Refund policy assumes monthly subscriptions and Rs 4,999 setup add-on.
   - Fix with one central pricing catalog and synchronized legal pages.

2. **Privacy policy does not match the current product**
   - It omits or under-specifies parties/customers, GSTIN, dispatch/challan data, stock sales, market runs, commercial receivables, payment receipts, referrals, Razorpay payments, Sentry monitoring, Upstash rate limiting, audit logs, tracking links, and broader WhatsApp logs.
   - It still emphasizes older yarn/yield modules in places.

3. **Data role framing is too simple**
   - The policy says Weavly acts as processor for factory owners. That is true for worker/factory operational data, but Weavly is likely a Data Fiduciary for account, billing, support, trial, security, analytics, website, and customer relationship data.

4. **Export/delete coverage is incomplete**
   - `app/(dashboard)/owner/settings/data-export.ts` covers many older tables but misses newer/active tables such as `parties`, `fabric_stock_lots`, `fabric_stock_sales`, `fabric_stock_movements`, `fabric_stock_inward_drafts`, `stock_discrepancies`, `market_runs`, `market_run_sales`, `commercial_receivables`, `commercial_payment_receipts`, `dispatch_package_lines`, `fabric_packages`, `subscription_plans`, `org_subscriptions`, `subscription_payments`, `referral_*`, `whatsapp_delivery_log`, `notification_consents`, and `razorpay_webhook_events`.
   - The UI does not currently expose a polished export/delete workflow in owner settings.

5. **DPA is not active for customers**
   - `/owner/settings/dpa` redirects to `/owner/settings`.
   - DPA tables and actions exist, but customer self-service DPA signing is not part of the active launch surface.

6. **Breach handling needs a real runbook**
   - Privacy policy says 72-hour breach notice. DPDP Rules 2025 require affected Data Principals to be informed without delay and Board intimation without delay, with detailed Board information within 72 hours. CERT-In directions separately require certain cyber incidents to be reported within 6 hours.
   - Current app code has breach reports/lockdown, but the legal runbook and production alerting are not enough yet.

7. **Security hardening has unresolved manual production actions**
   - `SECURITY_HARDENING_REPORT.md` requires production deployment of security migration, anon grant/RLS verification, secret rotation, Razorpay webhook setup, Supabase DB verification, and pg_net/cron secret cleanup.
   - Do not claim production security readiness until these are verified in production.

8. **Worker consent evidence is weaker than policy ambition**
   - Onboarding captures owner declaration for worker phone consent.
   - `worker_consent_artifacts` exists but is not integrated into the active onboarding flow.
   - Add downloadable/signed worker consent or direct worker consent capture for serious paid customers.

9. **Age/minor worker representation is not enforced**
   - Privacy policy says no under-18 data.
   - Worker creation does not require DOB or age declaration.
   - Add an owner declaration: "I confirm this worker is 18+ or legally permitted under applicable law", and decide whether DOB is necessary or excessive.

10. **Payroll compliance must be disclaimed and made configurable**
    - The app calculates payroll, advances, PF/ESI fields, pay dates, and settlements, but it should not claim statutory compliance.
    - Labour Codes became effective from November 21, 2025. Terms and app copy should reference applicable labour codes/current law, not just older acts.
    - `lib/wage-config.ts` contains fixed minimum wage values; these can go stale. Make legal wage thresholds configurable per state/role/date or remove claims from the app.

11. **Intermediary-law assumptions need lawyer review**
    - Current privacy/terms refer to IT Intermediary Guidelines and 180-day account retention. Weavly may be a SaaS/service provider rather than a classic intermediary. Keep retention where useful, but have counsel confirm the legal basis.

12. **Billing implementation is not launch-aligned**
    - `app/api/subscriptions/create/route.ts` accepts plan IDs and creates monthly subscriptions with `total_count: 12`.
    - It validates plan IDs server-side, which is good, but it does not support setup fee addons, annual plans, package metadata, or delayed start.

## 12. Pilot With Your Brother

### Pilot Principle

The pilot is not "launch". It is a controlled operational rehearsal with real workflow pressure and limited legal exposure.

Before pilot:

- Use a pilot agreement or at least a signed email with scope.
- Confirm who is the Data Fiduciary for worker/customer data.
- Get worker phone/WhatsApp consent if phone numbers are entered.
- Use test/simulated payments only.
- Decide whether data will be deleted, exported, or migrated after pilot.
- Make it explicit that payroll/statutory compliance remains the factory owner's responsibility.

### Pilot Setup Checklist

1. Create a fresh pilot org.
2. Confirm production mode: market-run, order-based, or both.
3. Add real machine types and machine count.
4. Add a small set of workers, with phone numbers only where consent exists.
5. Configure payroll cycle, pay date, and reminder window.
6. Configure factory cost profile.
7. Add one estimator template for a real fabric quality.
8. Start one quote/order or one market run.
9. Add supervisor account via join code.
10. Decide WhatsApp mode: simulation/manual fallback unless live Meta setup is done.
11. Disable real checkout unless Razorpay live/KYC is complete.

### Pilot Workflows That Must Work

| Workflow | Pass criteria |
| --- | --- |
| Onboarding | Owner can complete production mode, machines, workers, payroll config, trial/access. |
| Estimator | Real inputs produce a quote the owner believes. |
| Quote/order or market run | Owner can create the work item and see it in supervisor selection. |
| Supervisor shift log | Supervisor can log day/night production on phone in under 2 minutes per shift batch. |
| Corrections/access code | Protected correction works and is understandable. |
| Stock | Produced/sold meters make sense to the owner. |
| Dispatch | Pending dispatch, partial dispatch, receipt confirmation, and shortage/extra handling work. |
| Commercials | Revenue, receivables, payment status, and gross profit align with manual calculation. |
| Payroll | Preview, settlement, advances, deductions, and payment mode match manual hisaab. |
| WhatsApp/manual fallback | Payslip/advance/access-code messages either send or provide manual fallback without blocking writes. |
| Export | You can produce a full pilot data export manually even if app export remains incomplete. |
| Security | Owner cannot see another org; supervisor cannot access owner-only routes. |

### Pilot Evidence To Collect

- Time to onboard a factory.
- Time for supervisor to log a shift.
- Number of support calls per day.
- Manual payroll difference vs Weavly payroll.
- Manual stock/commercial difference vs Weavly.
- Confusing labels/screens.
- Willingness to pay setup fee.
- Which feature the owner would miss after turning off Weavly.
